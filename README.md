# Braiya-Nolan-YSPH-MPH-Thesis-
Supplemental Information for my Yale School of Public Health EMD MPH Thesis: Successive blood meal feeding and Wolbachia impact on the intra-host genetic diversity of dengue virus in Aedes Aegypti mosquitoes

Dengue virus (DENV) is a mosquito-borne RNA virus and a growing public health threat. Due to its error prone replication, single-nucleotide variants (iSNVs) are rapidly generated within the vector, leading to heterogeneity in viral populations, fitness, virulence, and transmissibility, including under vector contorl interventions. A major intervention is the global deployment of Wolbachia-infected mosquitoes; however, its effects on DENV inta-host diversity remain unclear. Additionally, frequent blood feeding may enhance viral dissemination and alter intra-host diversity. Here, I tested whether Wolbachia infection (wAlbB) and feeding behavior independently or interactively shape DENV-2 genetic variation. I deep-sequenced DENV-2 populations from bodies and legs of wild type and wAlbB Ae. aegypti mosquitoes following a single infectious blood meal or an additional non-infectious blood meal. High titer samples were identified by RT-qPCR and normalized for viral genome equivalents to reduce depth-driven bias, and analysed with iVar to call iSNVs (≥3% frequency and ≥400x depth). Diversity was quantified using iSNV and amino acid distances, Shannon entropy (Sn), and dN/dS ratios. Selected nonsynonymous iSNVs present in the global DENV-2 Nextstrain dataset were evaluated using Alphafold structural modeling. Overall, viral diversity did not differ significantly from wildtype, but wAlbB single-fed leg samples demonstrated a statistically higher iSNV frequency, indicating possible Wolbachia-induced selection. Structural predictions suggested that envelope substitution N124Y may affect envelope dimerization and thus, possibly transmission. This work provides insights into how feeding behavior and Wolbachia shape DENV-2 viral evolution within the mosquito, informing arbovirus evolution and intervention strategies.
